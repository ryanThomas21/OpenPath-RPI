In accordance with Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, our
information must be comparably accessible and usable between disabled 
and able-bodied members of the public. 
This list will also take inspiration from the Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines (WCAG) 2 requirements, despite our project not being an official
government project.
Reference website for 508 Guidelines: 
https://www.ada.gov/resources/small-entity-compliance-guide/#1-archived-web-content
Reference website for WCAG 2: 
https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/
----------------Ease of information access----------------------------------
We will ensure that the Google read-aloud functions properly on our website.
This includes a verbal transcription of the optimal route determined by our
program.
Visuals should use text cues alongside color cues in a consistent,
non-conditional manner.
Ensure proper color contrast ratio. A minimum of 4.5:1 is acceptable, with
lower exceptions dealing with large-scale text (18 point or 
14 point bold text on client end. Recommend avoiding due to this nebulous
nature regarding a normally stable color palette). Proper color contrast
ratio checkers can be found online.
Our website should also be able to scale reasonably with page zoom and
text-size adjustment features for computer and mobile devices respectively.
All user input must be given a title describing its purpose.
All site functions must be accessible without a mouse.
If needed, employ a programmatically determined website structure. That is,
any information that needs to be given in a specific order must be processed
by screen readers in that order. This can be accomplished through markup
language or through implementing an accessibility API.
